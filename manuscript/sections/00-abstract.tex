\begin{abstract}
Android apps persist outboxes and jobs that can outlive a user's withdrawal.
The enforcement problem is not just when to check a toggle: derived data must retain its source policy, and old data must remain distinguishable after consent is granted again.
\sys keeps durable source epochs and labels with every persisted copy and job input, and checks the app's stated contract before each bounded socket write, serialized with withdrawal.
We judge each workload against the contract it states: collection-only opt-out, restriction or deletion.
A \corpusN-app static screen identifies candidate deferred paths, not runtime violations; of the \promiseApps{} apps with a consent signal, \promisePending{} word their switch as sending or sharing data.
Lineage and epochs decide the lifecycle schedules: conventional checks fail through lost lineage, stale epochs and unchecked retries, while layered early checks that receive the same metadata had no confirmed violations, like the fence, on emulators and two rented phones.
Placement matters only when a withdrawal lands during an upload.
A check before the request let the socket accept bytes after the acknowledgement in \racePhoneObsPct{}\% of trials on a phone (\raceObsPct{}\% on an emulator); on a rented phone, synchronously cancelling in-flight requests let none follow it, like the fence.
They differ at the withdrawal's commit, after which the app's own policy state forbids the data: cancellation let the socket accept newly started writes in \dfCloseAll{} of \dfArmN{} trials from another process and in \dfCloseTwoAll{} from inside the sending process, which is not serialized with the writer; the fence, which rechecks each write under the withdrawal's lock and needs no registry of in-flight requests, accepted none.
Below the socket the designs converge: about one send buffer of accepted bytes leaves after the acknowledgement with a normal close, and with equal buffers a real app's server received a donation complete about as rarely with its HTTP client's cancellation as with the fence (\gbSixteenRx{} of \gbSixteenN{} against \gbFenceRx{} of \gbFenceN{}).
In that app on a rented phone, the stock build shared data collected during an opt-out once the user re-consented in \dfAirStockPK{} of \dfAirPKN{} trials, the fenced and layered builds in none.
Label mutants show that a valid but incomplete label bypasses any placement and that a test-time check against traced content catches it.
On a phone the fence used \gdCpuLo{} to \gdCpuHi{} times the early check's process CPU per MiB; we make no energy claim.
\end{abstract}
