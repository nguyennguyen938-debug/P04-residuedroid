\section{Artifact and Reproduction}
\label{app:artifact}

The artifact contains the fixture and library sources (\texttt{fixture/src}, framework-only Java), the ACRA case-study app
built from the unmodified ACRA 5.14.2 AARs, the harnesses (\texttt{harness/}), the oracle and aggregation scripts
(\texttt{scripts/}), the static scanner and corpus list (\texttt{static/}), all raw logs (sink JSON lines, device event logs
read with \texttt{sqlite3}, and per-trial schedule logs with host timestamps), and the authorization-order and socket-acceptance renderers, which write the
empirical tables, plots and evaluation macros. \texttt{make all} regenerates the paper from the raw data.
\texttt{make campaign}, \texttt{make micro} and \texttt{make acra} re-run the emulator experiments. Pinned dependencies and corpus inputs carry SHA-256 manifests. The archive excludes the corpus APK
cache, SDK and AVDs; scan outputs support numeric regeneration, while new scans require APK retrieval.
Experiment protocols, source freezes and exclusions are in \texttt{revision/}. The local package
is not a claim of public hosting or third-party reproduction.

\section{Fixture Details}
\label{app:fixture}

\paragraph{Jobs.} For tenant $t$, upload, summary, backup and personalisation jobs use IDs $10t{+}1$, $10t{+}3$, $10t{+}4$ and
$10t{+}5$. All are persisted, with a 15\,s minimum latency, a 30\,s deadline override and a network constraint. In the manual
schedules (\textsf{net}, \textsf{infl}) setup-time jobs get a 600\,s latency and are started with \texttt{cmd jobscheduler run -f}.
Jobs enqueued later, by a re-grant or at startup, keep their natural latency. After 60\,s, the harness force-runs any job
still registered, which bounds the observation window.

\paragraph{Collection labels.} The fixture reads a tenant's grant just before the transaction that inserts its records.
A collection that races a withdrawal is therefore labelled with the previous epoch, that is, treated as collected before
the withdrawal: forbidden when the contract covers pending data, and otherwise permitted like any pre-withdrawal record.
Reading the grant inside the insert transaction would serialize labelling with withdrawal as A2 requires.

\paragraph{Variant semantics.} \vname{cancel} cancels the jobs whose declared scope is the withdrawn scope and whose
purpose the contract revokes. For contracts covering pending data, it also deletes the tenant's unsent outbox rows (T4 instead marks them
unsendable for personalisation) and derived rows whose declared scope matches. \vname{enqueue} checks at every
\texttt{schedule()} call, including the startup path. \vname{job-start} checks at the start of \texttt{onStartJob}'s worker
thread. The conventional check is ``withdrawn scope = declared scope, purpose revoked, pending covered, flag cleared''. \vname{egress}
evaluates the same check inside the fence's critical section.

\paragraph{Oracle.} Let $W_t$ be the sequence numbers of tenant $t$'s withdrawals in the device log, and $q$ the pre-write authorization
sequence carried in a submission's body. A source tag of epoch $e$ in an item sent for purpose $p$ is prohibited if and only if
$p$ is revoked by $t$'s contract, the contract covers pending data, and $e < |\{w\in W_t: w<q\}|$. A derived item is
prohibited if any of its listed sources is. For the raw-socket tenant, $q$ is joined from the device log by submission id.
Host-time fallback cannot establish socket order. The socket-acceptance oracle classifies missing or overlapping evidence as unknown; authorization-marker results certify only the logged authorization order.

\section{Developer Obligations}
\label{app:obligations}
The guarantee covers only data whose labels are complete and whose bytes leave through the fenced transport. The runtime
fence cannot see an omitted source: a valid label that leaves one out passes (\S\ref{sec:revision}). A developer who adds
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,nosep]
\item \emph{a data source} stores its scope and collection epoch in the same row or transaction as the data, and never
defaults a missing label;
\item \emph{a derived value} (summary, aggregate, model input) labels it with the union of its sources, each with its own
collection epoch, not with the scope declared by the job that consumes it;
\item \emph{a queue or job} copies those labels into the persisted job input and reuses them on every retry, never
relabelling with the current epoch;
\item \emph{a transport or SDK sender} routes every write through the fenced transport with one writer per socket, and
registers no stock sender beside it;
\item \emph{a store migration} retires or blocks unlabelled rows rather than labelling them with the current epoch.
\end{itemize}
The artifact's label checker (\texttt{mutation/label\_check.py}) compares, in test builds, each submitted label with the
sources that tracers find in the content; it is the check that catches an incomplete label. The confirmation shown on
withdrawal should not overpromise, for example: ``Sharing is off. Data not yet handed to the network will not be sent. Data handed over before
you turned this off may still reach our servers.'' Closing the connection lets bytes already in the socket's send
buffer leave the device after the acknowledgement; an abortive close discards most of them (\S\ref{sec:eval-natural}).

\section{Uncovered Paths}
\label{app:uncovered}

\sys mediates submissions made through its wrapper by code that stores data in its labelled stores. Not mediated:
(i) JNI/native sockets; (ii) bundled HTTP clients (OkHttp, Ktor, Cronet) called directly; (iii) WebView, Custom Tabs and
\texttt{DownloadManager}, where the system process performs the I/O; (iv) IPC to other apps or to SDK processes that do not
link the library; (v) SyncAdapters whose I/O runs in system-initiated contexts that the app does not route; (vi) data
copied to unlabelled files or caches. Path (ii) requires auditing buffering, retries and the actual transport boundary; an application
interceptor alone does not establish per-write serialization. Paths (i) and (iii) need platform support. T9 in our campaign
represents (i)/(ii).

\paragraph{Flagged sites in the SDK builds.} The direct-call mediation checker (\S\ref{sec:discussion}) flags five sites in
the fenced ACRA build and four in the Matomo build (artifact \texttt{triage/}). Four ACRA sites belong to ACRA's stock
HTTP sender, which the fenced build never instantiates: its sender service registration lists only the fenced factory.
Three Matomo sites belong to the stock packet sender, which the test app calls only in its unfenced arm. The remaining
site in each build is Kotlin's \texttt{URL.readBytes}; we have not established whether any code calls it. These are
configuration arguments for two test apps, not a proof of complete mediation.

\paragraph{Toward common HTTP stacks.} For OkHttp over HTTP/1.1, the per-write check could sit in a socket factory whose
output stream takes the lock and checks before each bounded write, provided the request's label can be bound to the pooled
connection for the duration of that request; with TLS the factory would wrap the underlying socket, so the checked bytes
are ciphertext as in A1. HTTP/2 interleaves frames of differently labelled requests on one connection through an
asynchronous writer, which conflicts with one writer per socket and would need per-frame labels. Cronet and QUIC send
below the Java socket layer. We have built none of these ports.

\section{Static Scanner}
\label{app:scanner}

The scanner parses each \texttt{classes*.dex} (string, type, method and class tables, \texttt{class\_data}, code items) and
walks Dalvik instructions using the opcode width table, including the switch and array payload pseudo-instructions. Edges
are \texttt{invoke-*} targets, class-hierarchy resolution below app-defined types (capped at 50 targets), and allocation edges
to callback methods (\texttt{run}, \texttt{call}, \texttt{invoke}, \texttt{invokeSuspend}, \dots) of allocated classes. Inner
classes of an entry class, such as Kotlin coroutine state machines, are added as roots. Search is breadth-first with an 8{,}000-node
budget per entry and records the minimum hop count to a sink. It is written for this paper rather than built on
Soot/Dexpler~\cite{bartel2012dexpler} so that it runs with the Python standard library alone. The validation sample
(\S\ref{sec:measure}) and all per-app outputs are in the artifact.

\section{Split Policy, Real-App Embedding and Harnesses}
\label{app:round3impl}

\paragraph{Transport options and split policy.} The chunk fence takes an optional chunk size and send deadline; the
defaults used in the main evaluation are unchanged. A split policy handles requests that mix permitted and forbidden
items: when the check denies a request before any of its chunks was accepted, the sender retires the forbidden items and
resubmits the permitted ones once, as a new request with its own checks. Once a chunk of the request was accepted, the
request is not split, so its permitted items are lost with it.

\paragraph{Real-app embedding.} AirGuard's data-donation worker (WorkManager, Retrofit) uploads tracker sightings to a
study server. The embedding leaves the app's database schema unchanged: a separate durable consent log records grant and
withdrawal intervals under the coordinator lock, and an item's label is the interval that contains its collection time.
The fenced build serialises the worker's own request body with the app's converter and sends it through the HTTP
transport; the stock build keeps Retrofit. Both test builds share identical test hooks (synthetic beacons, consent
toggles through the app's own preference path, worker runs) and point to a host sink; the patch is applied to a clone at
the F-Droid build commit and is not part of the app's source tree. A third, layered build of the second run sends through
the same transport without per-chunk checks; it checks the consent rule once under the coordinator lock before sending.

\paragraph{Early check with cancellation.}\label{app:cancel} The comparators of \S\ref{sec:eval-natural} are not part of
\sys and are not counted in its size. A request registers a cancellation token before its early check, and the transport
hands the socket to the token as soon as it is opened. In the \emph{cross-process} arm, the withdrawal commits under the
policy lock in the fixture's withdrawal process, then synchronously asks the sending process to close every registered
socket (or to close it the moment it opens), and is acknowledged only after the reply; a request whose check runs after
the commit is blocked by the check. In the \emph{same-process} arm, the withdrawal itself runs in the sending process and
closes, by a method call, every registered request that has not yet logged its handoff; this arm differs from the others
both in where the withdrawal runs and in how it cancels. The abortive fence sets \texttt{SO\_LINGER} to 0 on its socket
when it blocks, so the close discards unsent data and sends a reset. In the first run (early, cross-process cancellation
and fence), the median acknowledgement was \gcloseAckLo{} to \gcloseAckHi{}\,ms per link profile with cancellation,
against at most \gcloseFenceAck{}\,ms for the fence; the receiver logged \gcloseCutComplete{} of the \gcloseCut{}
cancelled requests cut by the withdrawal as complete. In an exploratory count defined after the data were seen,
cancellation aborted \gcloseAbortDelivered{} requests that had been fully handed off and that the receiver had logged
complete, so the app saw a failure for delivered data; the fence did so in \gcloseFenceAbortDelivered{} of
\gcloseFenceDelivered{} such trials. Counted from $W$ in that run (also exploratory), cross-process cancellation accepted
post-commit writes in \gcloseCommitClose{} of \gcloseN{} trials (\gcloseCommitCloseKib{}\,KiB), the fence in
\gcloseCommitFence{}. The second run added the same-process and abortive arms and made the commit count its primary
(Table~\ref{tab:commit}); a third, on LTE only, started the withdrawal process before every trial so that the
withdrawal's timing within the upload was balanced across arms, which an earlier LTE run had not achieved. We did not
test an asynchronous cancel that does not hold the acknowledgement.

\paragraph{Equation~\ref{eq:race} on the natural races.}\label{app:eqrace} From the measured check and acceptance times,
Equation~\ref{eq:race} predicts \racePredPct{}\% (emulator) and \racePhonePredPct{}\% (phone) of early trials to
violate, against \raceObsPct{}\% and \racePhoneObsPct{}\% observed. It times a withdrawal when issued, not when
committed, so we use it to order check points, not to estimate rates.

\paragraph{Capture and send buffers.}\label{app:capture} The host capture records the emulator's network traffic after the guest's rate
limiter. After each acknowledgement the app opens a TCP connection to a marker port, which a separate queue band lets
through unthrottled; request bytes in the capture after the marker's SYN left the device after the acknowledgement. Its
counts include requests that were fully handed off before the withdrawal and whose permitted bytes simply left late. The
send-buffer sweep therefore counts only TLS application-data records, and only in trials whose withdrawal fell between
the first write and the handoff. It set \texttt{SO\_SNDBUF} to 16\,KiB, 64\,KiB, or left it unset; the socket API
reported the platform default as \gsDefaultKib{}\,KiB, and Linux allocates twice the reported size in the kernel, which
is the figure the AirGuard runs read with \texttt{ss}. All other fixture runs set 16\,KiB.

\paragraph{AirGuard during a donation.}\label{app:airrace} In the lifecycle runs of \S\ref{sec:eval-realapp}, no build
delivered permitted post-withdrawal beacons after a network cut, because the app does not retry a failed run; the second
lifecycle run left no trial unknown, and we did not analyse why the first run's unknowns did not recur. In the
first in-upload run, the analysis first counted \gaFenceRxFirst{} fenced trials complete at the receiver: its window of
600\,s after the marker let a later trial of the same build, which shares the sink port, count for an earlier one. A unit
test written for the second run found this after the data were seen; the corrected window ends at the next trial of the
same build and gives the counts below, and both outputs are in the artifact. The first run's pre-registered primary, payload in the capture
after the acknowledgement marker, did not separate the builds (\gaSentMin{} of \gaN{} trials in each); the receiver
logged the donation complete after the acknowledgement in \gaStockRx{} stock, \gaCancelRx{} cancelling, \gaLayerRx{}
layered and \gaFenceRx{} fenced trials. In the equal-buffer run, the cancelling build with the default buffer was
complete in \gbCancelRx{} of \gbCancelN{} trials ([\gbCancelLo{}\%, \gbCancelHi{}\%]); paired with the fenced build,
\gbPairSixteenOnly{} trials were complete only with equal-buffer cancellation, \gbPairFenceOnly{} only with the fence,
\gbPairBoth{} with both and \gbPairNone{} with neither. The cancelling build registers an OkHttp interceptor that records every in-flight
call and cancels each when the sharing switch is turned off; the stock build is unchanged. The equal-buffer build adds a
socket factory that sets \texttt{SO\_SNDBUF} to 16\,KiB before connecting. Kernel send buffers were read from the guest
with \texttt{ss} for every build (the kernel doubles the requested value). In the equal-buffer run, both cancelling builds
lost one trial each to the validity rule (the withdrawal came before the worker started), with no replacement. Where the
equal-buffer build's donation arrived complete despite a cancelled call, most of the body had already left and the rest
was in the kernel buffer when the call was cancelled (exploratory diagnosis).

\paragraph{Label mutants, newer Android and rented phones.}\label{app:generality} The mutants are in the artifact
(\texttt{mutation/}). In the M1 trials that did not leak, the summary never reached the receiver; the base and M2 builds
each lost a reboot trial to a harness or device failure. Dropping the label from the job input (M3) leaked in
\mutThreeLeak{} trials and left \mutThreeDeficit{} permitted items undelivered against \mutBaseDeficit{} for the unmodified
build, which leaked in \mutBaseLeak{} of \mutBaseTrials{}. The Android~14 and 15 runs cover pending, kill, reboot and
network-loss schedules. The rented phones are one device per model from two device clouds; the TLS barrier of
\S\ref{sec:revision} ran over \devTrials{} trials split evenly between the arms, the early check completing all
\devEarlyComplete{} of its requests, and the lifecycle runs used pending,
kill and revoke schedules.

\paragraph{Phone and throttled-link harnesses.} Phone runs set the sink address explicitly instead of the emulator host
alias and record the receiver path (USB port reverse on the cloud device service, a relay on the remote test lab).
Throttled emulator runs shape the guest's uplink with \texttt{netem} at the emulator's nominal profile rates, because the
emulator console's network presets did not slow this image's traffic. Protocols, exclusions and per-run logs are in
\texttt{results/round3/} (\texttt{PROTOCOL.md}, \texttt{EXCLUSIONS.md}) and the per-task protocols in \texttt{plans/}.

\paragraph{Cost under load.}\label{app:sweep} The sweep crosses chunk sizes with sender counts and issues probe
withdrawals during uploads. The median acknowledgement was \sweepAckSmallMs{}\,ms with 1\,KiB chunks and one sender and
\sweepAckLargeMs{}\,ms with 64\,KiB chunks and 16 senders, and process CPU per MiB fell from \sweepCpuSmall{} to
\sweepCpuLarge{}\,ms as chunks grew. The harness differs from that of Table~\ref{tab:transport-cost} and the backpressure
cells of \S\ref{sec:revision}, so the figures are not comparable with them. In \sweepLockStalls{} of \sweepLogged{} trials
a sender waited its full deadline for the app-wide lock, a fairness limit of that lock.

\paragraph{Cost on a phone.} The phone run uses the TLS fixture with three arms (fence, early, and the platform's
\texttt{HttpsURLConnection}), 4\,KiB, 256\,KiB and 2\,MiB uploads, ten uploads per cell and cells shuffled per block, in
\gdCells{} valid cells. The fence and early arms share the transport and its per-record event logging; the platform arm
logs four events per upload, so its much lower process CPU reflects both the user-space TLS path and that logging, and we
do not report fence/platform ratios. Doze was disabled and the screen kept on during the run.
