\subsection{Natural races}
\label{sec:eval-natural}
Only the in-flight lifecycle schedule withdraws while a request may be written, and it did not separate a check before
the request from the fence (Table~\ref{tab:matched}). We uploaded 32\,KiB, 256\,KiB and 2\,MiB payloads over links shaped to EDGE, 3G and LTE rates and withdrew at
a seeded uniform time during the upload, with no barrier, on the emulator and on a \racePhoneModel{} phone
(Android~\racePhoneAndroid{}) whose receiver ran on a shaped cloud host. The early variant checks once before writing,
the last point a layered early check covers (Table~\ref{tab:race}).

The early check let bytes follow the acknowledgement in every cell; the fence never did. In an exploratory
receiver-side count, the difference reaches the server: of the early trials whose withdrawal was acknowledged before the request was fully handed off, the receiver
still logged the complete request in \raceCutEarlyComplete{} of \raceCutEarly{} (emulator) and
\racePhoneCutEarlyComplete{} of \racePhoneCutEarly{} (phone); of the fenced trials cut that way, in
\raceCutFenceComplete{} of \raceCutFence{} and \racePhoneCutFenceComplete{} of \racePhoneCutFence{}.
Equation~\ref{eq:race} orders these check points but does not estimate their rates (Appendix~\ref{app:eqrace}).

\paragraph{Cancellation and the commit point.} Two later pre-registered runs, separate from Table~\ref{tab:race}, shared
blocks among five arms: early check, fence, abortive fence, and closing in-flight sockets after the withdrawal commits,
from its own process (\emph{cross-process}) or in the sending process for requests not yet handed off
(\emph{same-process}; Appendix~\ref{app:cancel}). Cancelling arms accepted bytes after the acknowledgement in
\gtrCancelAck{} trials, early in \gtrEarlyAck{} of \gtrN{}; at the commit $W$, Theorem~\ref{thm:sound}'s boundary, the
arms separate (Table~\ref{tab:commit}; an exploratory recount of Table~\ref{tab:race} from $W$ changes no cell).

Cross-process cancellation reaches the writer only after a message crosses processes, and writes started in that delay
were accepted: its \gtrCloseW{} pooled cases all came from LTE cells, where uploads are shortest, and in the separate LTE
run every trial whose withdrawal fell during the writes accepted one, \gpCloseExposed{} with cross-process cancellation
as \gpEarlyExposed{} with the early check. Same-process cancellation shortens the delay to a method call, but nothing
serializes it with the writer, so its zero bounds a rate rather than closing the window, and it needs a registry of
every in-flight request (\S\ref{sec:eval-realapp} shows one missed). What the commit requires is serialization with
each write, which the fence has by construction. On LTE the fence's p95 acknowledgement was
\gpFenceAckP{}\,ms (\gpAbortAckP{}\,ms abortive) against \gpCloseTwoAckP{}\,ms for same-process cancellation
(Appendix~\ref{app:cancel} has the cross-process kind).

\paragraph{After acceptance.} Accepted bytes still leave the device. A host capture behind the emulator's rate limiter,
with a marker connection after each acknowledgement, found request payload after the marker in \gtrCapLo{} to
\gtrCapHi{} of \gtrN{} trials per arm, fence included. Varying the send buffer (\gsN{} trials per cell), fenced trials cut during the writes sent a median of \gsFenceSmallLo{} to \gsFenceSmallHi{}\,KB of
TLS application data after the marker at a requested 16\,KiB, \gsFenceMidLo{} to \gsFenceMidHi{}\,KB at 64\,KiB and
\gsFenceDefHi{}\,MB at the platform default (reported as \gsDefaultKib{}\,KiB): each between the buffer size and twice
it, which Linux allocates. The abortive close left a median
of \gsAbortMed{} in every cell and at most \gsAbortMax{} bytes, less than the normal close in \gsAbortLess{} of \gsPairs{} pairs
(Appendix~\ref{app:capture}).

\subsection{A real app and mixed requests}
\label{sec:eval-realapp}
AirGuard, an open-source tracker detector, donates sightings through a WorkManager worker and Retrofit while ``Support
the project by sharing some anonymous data'' is enabled, a switch worded as sharing (\S\ref{sec:measure}). Our embedding
labels each item with the consent interval in which it was collected, kept in a durable log; an item collected during an
opt-out is labelled $\bot$, so Eq.~\ref{eq:rule} forbids it even after re-consent (Appendix~\ref{app:round3impl}). Running both builds in lockstep through the app's own
preference path, the stock build donated opt-out beacons after re-consent in \airStockViol{} of \airN{} trials (pending,
kill and reboot pooled) and the fenced build in \airFenceViol{} confirmed. The pre-registered oracle leaves
\airFenceUnknownV{} fenced trials unknown; ordering on the guest clock alone, an exploratory step, leaves
\airFenceUnknownVThree{}. Stock and fenced builds differ in both labels and placement, so this comparison does not
isolate placement. A separate run added a layered build with the same labels and one check before sending: stock
violated in \glayStockViol{} of \glayN{} trials, fenced in \glayFenceViol{}, layered in \glayLayerViol{}, \glayUnknown{} unknown; these schedules
withdraw before the next donation.

\paragraph{Withdrawal during a donation.} Two pre-registered runs withdrew while a 256\,KiB donation was uploading on a
3G-shaped link, adding a build that calls OkHttp's \texttt{Call.cancel()} on every in-flight call. Payload left after the
acknowledgement marker in \gaSentMin{} of \gaN{} trials of every build, and the receiver logged the donation complete
afterwards in \gaFenceRx{} fenced trials against \gaCancelRx{} cancelling, but OkHttp's kernel send buffer was
\gbKernDefKib{}\,KiB against \gbKernSmallKib{}\,KiB for the fence (Appendix~\ref{app:airrace}). With equal buffers, in
the same trials, the donation arrived complete (that run's primary) in \gbSixteenRx{} of \gbSixteenN{} cancelling trials (Wilson
[\gbSixteenLo{}\%, \gbSixteenHi{}\%]) and \gbFenceRx{} of \gbFenceN{} fenced ([\gbFenceLo{}\%, \gbFenceHi{}\%]): no clear
difference at the receiver. In \gbNoCallRx{} of them (exploratory), the withdrawal preceded OkHttp's registration of the
call and the whole body arrived; the fence has no such window.

\paragraph{Mixed requests and SDKs.}\label{sec:eval-acra} The split policy (\S\ref{sec:theory}) recovered the permitted
part of a mixed request in \splitPreRecovered{} of \splitTrials{} trials blocked before any chunk was accepted and in
\splitPostRecovered{} once one was; \splitAfterAck{} accepted bytes after the acknowledgement. Appendix~\ref{app:sdk}
covers ACRA~5.14.2 and Matomo~4.4.

\subsection{Label errors, newer Android, phones and cost}
\label{sec:eval-generality}
\paragraph{Label errors.} Each mutant corrupts one label propagation point. Labelling raw records with the current epoch
leaked in \mutTwoLeak{} of \mutTwoTrials{} trials, and keeping only a summary's newest source label in \mutOneLeak{} of
\mutOneTrials{}; dropping the label from the job input failed closed. A test-time checker that compares each label with
the sources traced in its content flagged \mutTwoFlagged{} of \mutTwoWrong{} and \mutOneFlagged{} of \mutOneWrong{}
mislabelled items. Runtime enforcement cannot see a valid but incomplete label; the test-time check can, given content
that carries source tracers as the fixture's does (Appendix~\ref{app:generality}).

\paragraph{Newer Android and rented phones.} On Android~14 and 15 emulators (\lifeFourteenTrials{} and
\lifeFifteenTrials{} paired trials), the enqueue-time baseline violated in \lifeFourteenEnq{} and \lifeFifteenEnq{}
trials, layered early checks in \lifeFourteenLayer{} and \lifeFifteenLayer{}, and the fence in \lifeFourteenFence{} and
\lifeFifteenFence{}. On two rented phones (\devModel{}; Android~\devAndroid{}), the fence accepted application ciphertext
after acknowledgement in \devFenceAfterAck{} TLS barrier trials and violated in \devLifeFenceViol{} of \devLifeFenceN{} lifecycle trials against
\devLifeEnqViol{} of \devLifeEnqN{} for the enqueue baseline (Appendix~\ref{app:generality}).

\paragraph{Cost.} A sweep of chunk size and sender count in a separate harness (Appendix~\ref{app:sweep}) put
the median acknowledgement between \sweepAckLargeMs{} and \sweepAckSmallMs{}\,ms, and some senders stalled on the app-wide lock. On the \racePhoneModel{} over Wi-Fi, in the
foreground (\gdUploads{} uploads of 4\,KiB to 2\,MiB, none failed), the TLS fence used \gdCpuLo{} to \gdCpuHi{} times the
early check's process CPU per MiB and \gdTimeLo{} to \gdTimeHi{} times its median upload time; the two differ only in
the check and lock around each write. The platform's power model gave the app a nonzero estimate in \gdEnergyNonzero{} of
\gdEnergyCells{} cells, so we have no energy reading.
