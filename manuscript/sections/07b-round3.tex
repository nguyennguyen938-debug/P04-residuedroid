\subsection{Natural races}
\label{sec:eval-natural}
Lifecycle schedules never withdraw while a request is written, so they cannot separate a check before the request from
the fence. We uploaded 32\,KiB, 256\,KiB and 2\,MiB payloads over links shaped to EDGE, 3G and LTE rates and withdrew at
a seeded uniform time during the upload, with no barrier, on the emulator and on a \racePhoneModel{} phone
(Android~\racePhoneAndroid{}) whose receiver ran on a shaped cloud host. The early variant checks once before writing,
the last point a layered early check covers (Table~\ref{tab:race}).

The early check let bytes follow the acknowledgement in every cell; the fence never did. The difference reaches the
server: of the early trials whose withdrawal was acknowledged before the request was fully handed off, the receiver
still logged the complete request in \raceCutEarlyComplete{} of \raceCutEarly{} (emulator) and
\racePhoneCutEarlyComplete{} of \racePhoneCutEarly{} (phone); of the fenced trials cut that way, in
\raceCutFenceComplete{} of \raceCutFence{} and \racePhoneCutFenceComplete{} of \racePhoneCutFence{}. A fenced upload cut
by a withdrawal ends with a send error and loses its permitted part unless it is split (\S\ref{sec:eval-realapp}).

\paragraph{Early check with cancellation.} A second pre-registered run added an arm whose withdrawal, after it commits
and before it is acknowledged, closes every in-flight socket. Bytes followed the acknowledgement in \gcloseEarlyViol{} of
\gcloseN{} early trials, \gcloseCloseViol{} with cancellation and \gcloseFenceViol{} fenced: an effective cancellation
closes the window (Theorem~\ref{thm:race}), with no confirmed difference from the fence. It needs a path to every
in-flight request, slows the acknowledgement and, in an exploratory count, aborted \gcloseAbortDelivered{} requests the
receiver had already logged complete (Appendix~\ref{app:cancel}).

Equation~\ref{eq:race}, applied to the measured check and acceptance times, predicts \racePredPct{}\% (emulator) and
\racePhonePredPct{}\% (phone) of early trials to violate, against \raceObsPct{}\% and \racePhoneObsPct{}\% observed. The
model lets a withdrawal act when issued, not when committed (an exploratory delay decomposition is in the artifact), so
we use it to order check points, not to estimate rates.

\subsection{A real app and mixed requests}
\label{sec:eval-realapp}
AirGuard, an open-source tracker detector, donates sightings through a WorkManager worker and Retrofit while ``Support
the project by sharing some anonymous data'' is enabled, a switch worded as sharing (\S\ref{sec:measure}). Our embedding
labels each item with the consent interval in which it was collected, kept in a durable log; an item collected during an
opt-out is labelled $\bot$, so Eq.~\ref{eq:rule} forbids it even after re-consent (Appendix~\ref{app:round3impl}). Running both builds in lockstep through the app's own
preference path, the stock build donated opt-out beacons after re-consent in \airStockViol{} of \airN{} trials (pending,
kill and reboot pooled) and the fenced build in \airFenceViol{} confirmed. The pre-registered oracle leaves
\airFenceUnknownV{} fenced trials unknown; ordering on the guest clock alone, an exploratory step, leaves
\airFenceUnknownVThree{}. Stock and fenced builds differ in both labels and placement, so this comparison does not
isolate placement. A second run added a layered build with the same labels and one check before sending: stock
violated in \glayStockViol{} of \glayN{} trials, fenced in \glayFenceViol{}, layered in \glayLayerViol{}, \glayUnknown{} unknown; these schedules
withdraw before the next donation. After a network cut no build delivers permitted post-withdrawal beacons, because the app does not retry a failed run.

\paragraph{Mixed requests.} With the split policy of \S\ref{sec:theory}, the transport recovered the permitted part of a
mixed request in \splitPreRecovered{} of \splitTrials{} trials when the fence blocked before any chunk was accepted, and
in \splitPostRecovered{} of \splitTrials{} once a chunk had been accepted, which the policy does not split.
\splitAfterAck{} trials accepted bytes after the acknowledgement.

\paragraph{SDK embeddings.}\label{sec:eval-acra} Stock ACRA~5.14.2 sent a queued crash report after reporting was
disabled in \acraStockLeak{} trials, under a setting that promises nothing about queued reports; ACRA and Matomo~4.4
embeddings with explicit contracts are in Appendix~\ref{app:sdk}.

\subsection{Label errors, newer Android, phones and cost}
\label{sec:eval-generality}
\paragraph{Label errors.} Each mutant corrupts one label propagation point (artifact \texttt{mutation/}). Labelling raw
records with the current epoch leaked in \mutTwoLeak{} of \mutTwoTrials{} trials; keeping only a summary's newest source
label leaked in \mutOneLeak{} of \mutOneTrials{} (in the rest, the summary never reached the receiver); the base and
M2 builds each lost a reboot trial to a harness or device failure. A test-time checker that compares each label with
the sources traced in its content, which requires content that carries source tracers as the fixture's does, flagged \mutTwoFlagged{} of \mutTwoWrong{} and
\mutOneFlagged{} of \mutOneWrong{} mislabelled items. Dropping the label from the job input failed closed
(\mutThreeLeak{} leaking trials; \mutThreeDeficit{} permitted items undelivered against \mutBaseDeficit{} for the
unmodified build, which leaked in \mutBaseLeak{} of \mutBaseTrials{}). Runtime enforcement cannot see a valid but
incomplete label; the test-time check can.

\paragraph{Newer Android.} On Android~14 and 15 emulators (\lifeFourteenTrials{} and \lifeFifteenTrials{} paired trials
over pending, kill, reboot and network loss), the enqueue-time baseline violated in \lifeFourteenEnq{} and
\lifeFifteenEnq{} trials, layered early checks in \lifeFourteenLayer{} and \lifeFifteenLayer{}, and the fence in
\lifeFourteenFence{} and \lifeFifteenFence{}: zero against zero for the last two, as on Android~13.

\paragraph{Rented phones.} On two rented phones (\devModel{}; Android~\devAndroid{}; one device per model, from two
device clouds), the TLS barrier of \S\ref{sec:revision} repeated over \devTrials{} trials split evenly between the arms:
the early check completed all \devEarlyComplete{} requests, and the fence accepted application ciphertext after
acknowledgement in \devFenceAfterAck{} trials. In pending, kill and revoke schedules, the enqueue baseline violated in
\devLifeEnqViol{} of \devLifeEnqN{} trials and the fence in \devLifeFenceViol{} of \devLifeFenceN{}.

\paragraph{Cost under load.} With probe withdrawals during uploads, the median acknowledgement was \sweepAckSmallMs{}\,ms
(1\,KiB chunks, one sender) and \sweepAckLargeMs{}\,ms (64\,KiB, 16 senders), and process CPU per MiB fell from
\sweepCpuSmall{} to \sweepCpuLarge{}\,ms as chunks grew (a separate harness, not comparable with
Table~\ref{tab:transport-cost}). In \sweepLockStalls{} of \sweepLogged{} trials a sender waited its full deadline for the app-wide
lock, a fairness limit of that lock.
