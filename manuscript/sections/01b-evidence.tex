\paragraph{Evidence.} The evaluation separates information requirements from final placement.
The \corpusN-app static screen identifies candidate deferred paths without inferring violations, and the wording of the
\promiseApps{} apps with a consent signal shows which contracts they state (\S\ref{sec:measure}).
The ten-tenant fixture exposes source-lineage loss in derived summaries (T1), stale epochs after re-grant (T5), and unchecked retry paths (T8); their frequencies depend on the workload mix (\S\ref{sec:eval}).
When layered early checks receive the same durable metadata, they have no confirmed violations in lifecycle schedules on Android~13, 14 and 15 emulators and on two rented phones: there, lineage and epochs rather than placement decide the outcome.
Withdrawals that land during an upload separate placements: on shaped links, a check before the request admits bytes after the acknowledgement in \raceObsPct{}\% of emulator trials and \racePhoneObsPct{}\% on a phone, the fence in none; counted from the withdrawal's commit, cancellation from another process still accepts newly started writes, while the fence and same-process cancellation accept none (\S\ref{sec:eval-natural}).
Below the socket the designs converge: about one send buffer of accepted bytes leaves after the acknowledgement, and in AirGuard, a real app, OkHttp's cancellation with an equal send buffer did about as well as the fence at the server (\S\ref{sec:eval-realapp}).
In AirGuard's lifecycle runs, the stock build shares opt-out data after re-consent in \airStockViol{} of \airN{} trials, the fenced build in \airFenceViol{} confirmed (\airFenceUnknownV{} unknown) and, in a second run, a layered early build in \glayLayerViol{} of \glayN{}.
Label mutants, newer Android, three phones and cost runs bound what this evidence covers (\S\ref{sec:eval-generality}).
